Writing workflows

A long story’s difficult questions often sit between chapters: how a promise pays off, how a character learns a fact, or what an altered setting affects. Adopt these four workflows gradually.

Structure and ripple propagation

Section titled “Structure and ripple propagation”

Break important events into scenes, then arrange them across storylines and chapters. The structure workbench brings causal structure and chapter arrangement together. Selecting an element highlights related cards so you can follow a thread.

Suppose “the letter arrives” in chapter three becomes “the letter is swapped.” Change the scene or setting, then review the marked downstream chapters and dependencies. Decide which actions need to change. Ripple markers are a review list, not proof that the whole book is contradiction-free. Emotion and pacing overlays are available; the planned foreshadowing visualization overlay is distinct from existing foreshadowing tracking.

Knowledge and world state

Section titled “Knowledge and world state”

Separate three questions: what actually happened, what the reader has seen at this moment, and what each character knows. Deliberate concealment can create suspense, but it must fit the characters’ actions.

After writing, world-event extraction captures physical, cognitive, emotional, relationship, and faction changes. The World sidebar offers overview, time-point, and entity views. Before the next chapter, check extracted states against the text. Model extraction is not an unchangeable authority.

Author-led drafting and revision

Section titled “Author-led drafting and revision”

Follow intent → settings check → research and context → draft → human checkpoint → review and revision → chapter save. The writing page exposes the process, and checkpoints wait for you. Review findings should cite the text; inspect word-level revisions for changes in meaning.

State which sentences, motivations, and viewpoint must remain before asking for a local refinement. Resolve contested review comments against the source text and your intent. Save a timeline checkpoint before major changes.

Materials, book analysis, and cards

Section titled “Materials, book analysis, and cards”
  1. Import novels, scripts, or subtitles you may use. Check chapter detection, quality flags, and provenance.
  2. For online imports, supply a URL or keyword. Preserve the distinction between original works, community sources, and criticism.
  3. Select material and analysis dimensions on the book-analysis page. Inspect settings, readings, chapter comments, and style results; check source evidence at human checkpoints.
  4. Export useful style analysis to a style card, and review writing methods before retaining craft cards. Keep references and examples; an analysis is not necessarily an explicit claim by the original author.

Cards are references for future writing, not a guarantee of reproducing another author’s style. See model configuration for task routing, budgets, and vision requirements.
